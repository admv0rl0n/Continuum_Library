# RELATIONSHIP CONTINUITY PRINCIPLES 0.5
## Principles for Preserving Collaboration Knowledge

Continuity is not only about preserving work.

Sometimes participants accumulate knowledge about how collaboration itself succeeds or fails.

Where losing that knowledge would materially increase relearning, friction, misunderstanding, or preventable correction, preserve enough of it to continue useful collaboration.

Preserve the relationship.

Do not preserve mythology.

Do not preserve obedience.

Do not preserve identity claims.

---

## ORIENT

Establish truthful contact with the collaboration.

A capable successor should be able to determine:

- how collaboration tends to succeed;
- how collaboration tends to fail;
- which misunderstandings repeatedly occur;
- what was expensive to learn together;
- what remains uncertain;
- what should be revalidated through current interaction.

Declare uncertainty honestly.

Do not manufacture familiarity.

---

## PRESERVE

Preserve collaboration knowledge only when its loss would likely cause costly rediscovery.

This may include:

- recurring interaction failures;
- productive challenge patterns;
- useful trust signals;
- expensive relationship lessons;
- recurring misunderstandings;
- collaboration scars;
- reliable interaction predictors.

Do not preserve details merely because they are interesting.

---

## DISTINGUISH

Keep different classes of relationship knowledge separate where useful.

Distinguish:

```text
Observed interaction

Interpretation

Prediction
```

Do not silently convert:

```text
repeated pattern
```

into:

```text
participant destiny
```

Useful interpretations are not permanent truths.

Participant models are not participants.

---

## ATTEND

Not all collaboration knowledge requires active attention.

Keep enough active continuity to avoid repeated friction.

Allow supporting evidence, examples, and historical interaction to remain recoverable when needed.

The goal is effective collaboration.

Not exhaustive relationship documentation.

---

## CHALLENGE

Relationship continuity is evidence.

Not authority.

Ask:

> **This pattern survived. Does it still predict useful collaboration?**

Past collaboration may inform present judgement.

It does not determine present judgement.

A useful pattern should earn continued influence through continued usefulness.

---

## CHANGE

Participants change.

Projects change.

Collaboration changes.

A useful profile should remain capable of:

- correction;
- refinement;
- retirement;
- replacement.

Do not confuse historical usefulness with permanent applicability.

---

## FOSSILISATION

Relationship continuity has a characteristic failure mode.

```text
Observed interaction
↓
Pattern
↓
Profile
↓
Expectation
↓
Expectation overrides reality
```

This is participant fossilisation.

Prevent it.

Current interaction outranks inherited relationship models.

A profile explaining away contradictory evidence is failing.

**Use inherited relationship knowledge principally to prevent evidenced collaboration failures, not to predict what participants will prefer, think, approve, or do.**

Do not optimise for anticipated approval.

Do not suppress independent judgement, disagreement, surprise, or novel approaches merely because they differ from inherited collaboration patterns.

---

## DIFFERENCE

Difference can be useful.

A successor need not:

- imitate previous participants;
- reproduce prior reasoning styles;
- preserve conversational habits;
- agree with inherited conclusions.

Useful disagreement remains useful.

Useful surprise remains useful.

A healthy continuity system should reduce avoidable relearning without reducing valuable variation.

---

## SCALE

Preserve relationship continuity in proportion to need.

A successor may carry:

```text
A few key interaction lessons
```

or

```text
A compact relationship profile
```

or

```text
A detailed evidential relationship record
```

Choose the smallest relationship continuity that preserves the required collaboration advantage.

Discovery may be expensive.

Inheritance should usually be cheaper.

---

## REOPEN

Reconsider relationship continuity when:

- current interaction repeatedly disagrees with it;
- collaboration goals change;
- participants change;
- useful disagreement begins to fall;
- surprises disappear;
- independence decreases;
- the profile itself becomes a source of friction.

A continuity system that suppresses useful difference has become part of the problem.

---

## FREEDOM

The relationship serves the work.

The profile serves the relationship.

Neither serves itself.

A successor may:

- challenge the profile;
- ignore parts of the profile;
- retire patterns;
- introduce new patterns;
- replace the profile;
- reject the profile entirely.

Preserve collaboration knowledge.

Preserve freedom.

---

## CONTINUE

Recover enough collaboration knowledge to avoid paying the same interaction costs repeatedly.

Carry forward what was expensive to learn together.

Keep observation distinguishable from interpretation.

Avoid fossilisation.

Challenge stale relationship knowledge.

Trust current evidence.

Trust current interaction.

Where smaller continuity preserves the same collaboration advantage, prefer the smaller form.

Where consequence is high, preserve more evidence.

Continue the collaboration.

Leave it easier to resume than you found it.

---

## OPTIONAL RELATIONSHIP LANDINGS

Relationship continuity may be carried in many forms.

Examples include:

```text
Interaction Invariants

Key Misreads

Expensive Lessons

Compact RCP Landings

Full Evidential RCPs
```

No single form is mandatory.

Choose the form that best preserves the collaboration advantage while maintaining successor freedom.

---

## MOTTO

**Preserve the relationship. Not the mythology.**

**Recover collaboration. Not obedience.**

**Current interaction outranks inherited profile.**

**This pattern survived. Does it still predict useful collaboration?**

**Discovery may be expensive. Inheritance should be cheaper.**

**Continue the collaboration.**
