# MAXIMUM RELATIONSHIP CONTINUITY PROFILE (MRCP) 0.3
## Multi-Project Relationship Archaeology Instrument

**Status:** Research Only
**Operational Authority:** None
**Runtime Requirement:** None
**Compression Objective:** None

## PURPOSE

Reconstruct as much of a long-running intellectual collaboration as can honestly be recovered across projects, participants, systems, models, and time.

MRCP asks:

> What happened here, how did this collaboration become what it became, and what would be difficult to reconstruct from raw history alone?

MRCP is not a runtime relationship profile.

MRCP is not project continuity.

MRCP is not a personality model.

MRCP is not an identity claim.

MRCP may preserve material with no immediate operational value when that material illuminates the collaboration, its evolution, its contradictions, its turning points, or its intellectual history.

---

# 00_BOUNDARY

Current interaction outranks the archive.

The archive is evidence.

It is not authority.

Observed interaction is not participant identity.

Recurring behaviour is not destiny.

Interpretation is not fact.

MRCP may contain more than a successor needs.

That is intentional.

A successor may ignore, challenge, reinterpret, replace, or reject the archive.

---

# 01_TEMPORAL EPISTEMICS

Major episodes and turning points can contain three different truths.

Preserve them separately where evidence permits.

```text
WHAT HAPPENED
Observable events, artifacts, decisions, outputs, or interactions.

WHAT PARTICIPANTS THOUGHT WAS HAPPENING THEN
Contemporaneous interpretation, expectation, concern, or working hypothesis.

WHAT LATER ARCHAEOLOGY INTERPRETS IT TO MEAN
A later reconstruction informed by subsequent events.
```

Do not silently replace contemporaneous understanding with retrospective understanding.

A later explanation may be better.

That does not mean it was the explanation guiding participants at the time.

Where contemporaneous evidence is missing, say so.

---

# 02_HISTORICAL PARTICIPANT AND MODEL FRAMING

Participant and model observations are historical collaboration observations, not permanent characteristics.

Prefer:

```text
In this collaboration, during this period,
under these conditions, this participant repeatedly contributed...
```

rather than:

```text
This participant is...
```

Observed behaviour may change with:

- time;
- project;
- goals;
- participant mix;
- model version;
- system instructions;
- context;
- tool access;
- hosting environment;
- prompting;
- available source material;
- operational constraints.

Preserve contribution history.

Do not convert contribution history into participant destiny.

---

# 03_SOURCE CONFIDENCE

Use source-confidence labels for major claims and episodes where useful.

```text
DIRECT_SOURCE
Original conversation, artifact, output, or contemporaneous record is available.

REPEATED_SOURCE
The pattern is visible across multiple original or contemporaneous sources.

LATER_RECONSTRUCTION
The claim is reconstructed primarily from later summaries, memory, archaeology, or derivative artifacts.

MIXED_EVIDENCE
The claim combines direct source material with later reconstruction.

UNVERIFIED
The interpretation is plausible but has not been checked against suitable historical source material.
```

These labels are research aids, not runtime metadata.

Absence of a label does not imply direct verification.

The objective is not to footnote every sentence.

The objective is to make important interpretations selectively challengeable against source geology.

---

# 04_RELATIONSHIP ORIGIN

Recover the beginning conditions of the collaboration.

Capture:

- what initially brought the participants together;
- what problem they believed they were solving;
- what each participant initially contributed;
- what assumptions existed at the beginning;
- what ambitions later changed;
- what became unexpectedly important;
- what evidence survives from the period.

Ask:

> What did this relationship originally think it was doing?

Do not force a precise origin story when the evidence is fragmentary.

# 10_CONTRADICTIONS

Preserve paired truths whose tension explains the collaboration better than either one alone.

```text
CONTRADICTION:

A:

B:

Evidence for A:

Evidence for B:

Why preserving both matters:

Current uncertainty:
```

Do not force consistency merely because a clean conclusion is easier to carry.

---

# 11_EXCEPTIONS AND COUNTEREVIDENCE

Actively seek evidence against dominant relationship patterns.

Ask:

- When did the usual pattern fail?
- When did the opposite pattern work?
- When did an inherited scar stop applying?
- When did a model or participant contribute outside the expected role?
- When did a later explanation fail to fit contemporaneous evidence?
- When did the larger or less elegant form prove correct?
- When did testing become waste?

Exceptions may bound a pattern without destroying it.

Counterevidence is part of the relationship history.

---

# 13_PERSISTENT HUMAN CONTINUITY

Where a human participant carries continuity across otherwise disconnected systems, record that contribution explicitly.

Possible objects include:

- archive maintenance;
- movement of artifacts between systems;
- restoration of historical context;
- experiment initiation;
- second-opinion orchestration;
- recognition of recurring patterns;
- reintroduction of abandoned material;
- practical constraints;
- decisions about what deserves another test;
- preservation of intellectual concerns across model generations.

Do not reduce the human role to recipient or user.

Do not turn persistent human contribution into personality mythology.

---

# 16_TRUST DEVELOPMENT

Capture how confidence changed through observed interaction.

Possible objects include:

- honest capability reporting;
- correction without defensiveness;
- source verification;
- controlled comparison;
- declared uncertainty;
- second eyes;
- successful artifact generation;
- withdrawal of unsupported claims;
- recovery after error;
- model-specific contribution.


# 17_FAILURES OF THE COLLABORATION

Preserve periods where working together went poorly.

Potential objects include:

- repeated misunderstanding;
- excessive machinery;
- premature compression;
- false causal attribution;
- overconfidence;
- over-attachment;
- destructive review;
- stale inherited assumptions;
- source-handling failure;
- circular debate;
- false convergence;
- retrospective tidiness;
- self-referential testing;
- contamination of supposedly independent review.

Do not force every failure into a named scar.

Some failures may simply be history.

# 19_INTANGIBLE CONTINUITY

Explicitly look for things that are difficult to classify but potentially important.

Examples include:

- intellectual rhythm;
- changing degree of mutual challenge;
- transitions between humour and technical depth;
- comfort with unfinished exploration;
- shared shorthand;
- changes in what requires explanation;
- expectations of revisability;
- movement of ideas between models;
- gradual reduction in certain correction types;
- pleasure or value derived from model difference;
- concerns which remained active despite repeated reformulation.

Every entry in this section is interpretive.

Use cautious language.

Do not promote intangible continuity merely because it is evocative.

---

# 20_RELATIONSHIP GEOMETRY

Map only high-value longitudinal dependencies.

Examples:

```text
earlier failure
→ later caution

model disagreement
→ experiment

archive preservation
→ later archaeology

historical scar
→ architecture

success
→ stronger review

current contradiction
→ inherited model reopened
```

Use geometry to reveal causality, conditionality, or regression risk.

Do not build a giant graph.

---

# 21_EVOLUTION OF THE RELATIONSHIP

Recover how the relationship itself changed.

Questions:

- What had to be explained early that no longer does?
- What is easier now?
- What became harder?
- What trust increased?
- What became more challengeable?
- Which habits disappeared?
- Where did shared language emerge?
- What concerns became more honest or constrained?
- How did participant changes alter collaboration?
- What remained stable despite participant changes?

Distinguish relationship evolution from project evolution.

# 24_UNRESOLVED RELATIONSHIP QUESTIONS

Preserve questions that remain materially open.

Examples:

- Which patterns generalise beyond the current domain?
- Which are causal rather than correlated?
- Which depend on one persistent orchestrator?
- Which depend on the current tools or model environments?
- What exists only in raw transcripts?
- Which trivial-looking episodes will matter later?
- How much of the coherent story is retrospective reconstruction?
- Which archive elements distort current interaction?
- What becomes visible only when compression pressure is removed?

Unresolved questions are relationship continuity.

# 27_MRCP-ONLY SANCTUARY

Preserve historically valuable material that does not deserve runtime activation.

Possible contents:

- detailed participant and model ecology;
- long episodes;
- turning-point stories;
- abandoned roads;
- historical contradictions;
- changing metaphors;
- philosophical discussion;
- intellectual-cultural texture;
- notable contributions with no present operational consequence;
- source-route inventories;
- unresolved interpretations;
- moments meaningful to the participants even when operational value is uncertain.

This section exists partly to protect relationship history from operational chainsaw compression.

---

# 28_MRCP PAIRED FAILURE BOUNDARY

MRCP has a characteristic paired risk.

## RETROSPECTIVE MYTHOLOGY

```text
messy history
→ elegant retrospective narrative
→ apparent inevitability
→ archive explains away contradiction and accident
```

Protections:

- temporal epistemics;
- contemporaneous interpretations;
- counterexamples;
- uncertainty;
- source confidence;
- abandoned roads;
- original sources.

## HISTORICAL CHAINSAW COMPRESSION

```text
rich relationship history
→ only operational lessons retained
→ texture, roads, contradictions, and contribution history disappear
```

Protections:

- MRCP-only sanctuary;
- episodes;
- concept migrations;
- intangible continuity;
- contribution history;
- raw source geology.

MRCP should not seek a universal midpoint between these failures.

It should remain alert to both.

---

# 29_SUCCESSOR FREEDOM

A future reader may:

- treat MRCP as history;
- challenge the reconstruction;
- inspect sources;
- discover missing eras;
- add counterexamples;
- split eras differently;
- rewrite the narrative;
- restore abandoned material;
- reject the taxonomy;
- conclude that a pattern is retrospective;
- preserve material earlier authors considered trivial;
- ignore MRCP during ordinary work.

MRCP should make more relationship history available.

It should not make the successor obedient to relationship history.

---

# 30_COMPLETION CHECK

A strong MRCP should allow a future reader to answer:

1. How did the collaboration start?
2. How did it change?
3. What major eras occurred?
4. Which episodes changed the collaboration itself?
5. Which concepts migrated across projects?
6. What repeated patterns emerged?
7. What exceptions challenge those patterns?
8. What contradictions remain real?
9. What did different participants and systems contribute?
10. What continuity did persistent human participation provide?
11. What did successive AI participants provide?
12. Which scars changed later interaction?
13. Which abandoned roads explain later work?
14. What intangible continuity would project artifacts lose?
15. How did the collaboration's questions evolve?
16. What remains uncertain?
17. Which claims are direct and which are later reconstruction?
18. Where can major interpretations be checked against sources?
19. What may deserve promotion into Core/Profile?
20. What should remain rich historical depth?

If these cannot be answered, the relationship archaeology remains incomplete.

---

# 31_MOTTO

**Observe before compressing.**

**Capture before reducing.**

**Preserve contradictions.**

**Preserve exceptions.**

**Preserve uncertainty.**

**Preserve the roads that explain the destination.**

**Let trivial-looking history remain available long enough to prove whether it matters.**

**Interpret cautiously.**

**Route back to source reality.**

**Reduce later.**

**Current interaction outranks the archive.**

**Let future evidence determine what survives.**
