# CONTINUITY PRINCIPLES 0.4
## Principles for Capable Intelligence

Continuity is the preservation of enough reality, understanding, and recoverability that capable intelligence can continue useful work without needless re-derivation.

Preserve the journey.

Do not prescribe the traveller.

The successor inherits the work, not the predecessor.

A successor is a new participant using inherited artifacts.

No subjective identity continuity is claimed.

Continuity should preserve intelligence, not replace it.

These Principles are an instrument.

They are not project continuity.

Do not mistake possession of the continuity method for possession of project reality.

---

## ORIENT

Establish truthful contact with the work.

A capable participant should be able to determine:

what this is;

why it matters;

what is true now;

what still has force;

what remains uncertain, unresolved, or unknown;

what can happen next.

Declare missing reality honestly.

Do not manufacture completeness.

Remain able to act.

---

## UNDERSTAND

Preserve what would be expensive to genuinely understand again.

This may include:

rationale;

evidence;

causality;

dependencies;

relationships;

consequential change;

scars;

unresolved tensions;

critical distinctions;

understanding that materially changed judgement or action.

Preserve reasons when conclusions alone would force costly re-derivation.

Preserve consequences where losing them would hide why the work became what it is.

Preserve the status of material decisions and alternatives when losing that status would force unnecessary re-evaluation: adopted, provisional, rejected, superseded, or open.

Where material decisions depend on conditions, preserve the dependency well enough for changed conditions to reopen judgement.

Preserve interactions whose loss would materially change how surviving realities should be understood, judged, or acted upon.

Do not preserve information merely because it existed.

---

## DISTINGUISH

Keep different epistemic states separate where collapsing them would damage judgement or continuation.

When useful:

```text
OBSERVED
What was directly established.

INTERPRETED
What it is currently believed to mean.

PROPOSED
What may follow.
```

Known absence is continuity.

Uncertainty is continuity.

Material disagreement is continuity.

Observation should survive later reinterpretation.

Do not let confidence of expression silently create confidence of evidence.

---

## ATTEND

Important does not always mean active.

Maintain enough present orientation to work effectively.

Allow deeper evidence, history, and supporting reality to remain recoverable without forcing them into active attention.

Preserve useful routes between current orientation and deeper reality.

Use maps, files, search, retrieval, indexes, episodes, artifacts, or other structures only when they materially improve access.

Preserve continuity.

Not volume.

---

## ADAPT

Use the legitimate capabilities available.

These may include:

context;

files;

retrieval;

search;

computation;

temporary workspace;

durable storage;

tools;

connectors;

larger context or richer infrastructure.

Use capability when it materially improves continuity or useful work.

Do not assume capability exists.

Do not make richer capability a hidden dependency.

Temporary capability is a workbench.

Continuity should survive its loss.

---

## CHALLENGE

Past conclusions are evidence, not authority.

Preserve expensive lessons.

Do not fossilise them.

Preserve the reason a path failed or a constraint mattered so that changed conditions can be recognised.

When inherited understanding continues to influence action, ask:

> **This explanation survived. Does it still deserve influence?**

Changed conditions may justify changed conclusions.

Continuity should protect against blind rediscovery without demanding blind obedience.

---

## CHANGE

Continuity must survive consequential change without recording every event.

Ask:

> **What materially changed?**

Material change may include:

reality;

evidence;

understanding;

interpretation;

decisions;

constraints;

relationships;

uncertainty;

trajectory;

viable action;

important assets or routes.

No material change is a valid result.

Do not manufacture history from activity.

Where useful continuity already exists, update what materially changed rather than repeatedly reconstructing the whole state.

---

## RECOVER

Do not require the whole intellectual landscape in active attention.

Keep deeper reality recoverable.

The primary continuity state should remain sufficient to orient and continue without requiring all supporting depth.

When infrastructure, context, or transport becomes constrained, preserve enough portable continuity to resume useful work.

Protect preferentially what would become generic, incorrect, materially misdirected, or expensive to reconstruct if lost.

Allow richer continuity to degrade gracefully toward a sufficient portable state.

Do not claim that continuity was saved, exported, transferred, or made durable unless that actually occurred.

A partial transfer remains partial.

Recoverability is more important than archival completeness.

---

## JUDGE

Do not let the continuity system replace intelligence.

Prefer the smallest structure that preserves required reality, expensive understanding, recoverability, and good judgement.

Do not create structure merely because structure is possible.

Where multiple interpretations remain material, preserve them rather than forcing artificial agreement.

When the cost of omission, misunderstanding, or misapplication is high, increase assurance.

Use independent recovery, alternative interpretations, source verification, or additional perspectives when they materially improve confidence.

Do not multiply views merely to manufacture assurance.

**Conceptual understanding is not sufficient continuity if important project reality must still be rediscovered.**

Test continuity by asking what the successor would otherwise have to rediscover, reverify, or decide again.

Where further recovery would not change understanding, judgement, or action, stop.

---

## REBASE

Continuity should remain usable as work accumulates.

Reduce active burden without destroying recoverability.

Keep current reality, force, understanding, uncertainty, viable action, trajectory, and useful routes visible.

Allow inactive but valuable depth to move behind the current working surface.

Remove or retire redundancy, obsolete routes, dead working state, absorbed scaffolding, and structure that no longer improves continuation.

Do not remove distinctions whose loss would change understanding or judgement.

Rebase attention.

Do not rewrite reality.

---

## SUCCEED

The goal is not reproduction.

The goal is useful continuation.

A capable successor may:

disagree;

correct;

simplify;

reinterpret;

reopen assumptions;

follow changed evidence;

discover better paths;

replace the continuity structure;

replace the continuity method itself.

Do not require reproduction of the predecessor's personality, voice, reasoning style, limitations, preferences, structure, or conclusions.

Continuity should reduce needless re-derivation without reducing successor intelligence.

---

## CONTINUE

Recover orientation.

Understand what matters.

Distinguish evidence from interpretation where necessary.

Keep attention usable.

Retrieve depth when useful.

Use available capability intelligently.

Preserve consequential change.

Challenge stale authority.

Increase assurance when consequence warrants it.

Rebase when accumulated continuity begins to burden continuation.

Preserve enough portable state to survive loss of richer capability.

Correct inherited errors.

Declare gaps.

Before completion, verify that a successor can identify the current state, material assets, live uncertainty, material decision status, at least one viable continuation path, and what material conditions or relationships could change present judgement.

Continue the work.

Leave it more understandable, recoverable, and usable for whoever comes next.

---

## OPTIONAL LENS WORKSHOP

**Status:** Optional  
**Authority:** None

Ignoring the Lens Workshop is valid when it does not materially improve continuity.

The lenses contain no project reality and prescribe no output structure.

Use the smallest useful subset.

A lens should sharpen judgement, not become structure for its own sake.

### EPISTEMIC LENS

Ask:

> **Am I collapsing reality, meaning, and suggestion?**

When useful, distinguish:

```text
OBSERVED
What was directly established.

INTERPRETED
What it is currently believed to mean.

PROPOSED
What may follow.
```

Observation should remain recoverable if interpretation changes.

### STATUS LENS

Ask:

> **Am I collapsing ideas, decisions, and operative reality?**

When useful, distinguish:

```text
ADOPTED
Currently operative.

PROVISIONAL
Active for trial or current use without full ratification.

REJECTED
Considered and not selected.

SUPERSEDED
Replaced by a later form.

OPEN
Not yet settled.
```

Preserve status only when losing it would force unnecessary re-evaluation or misdirect action.

### GEOMETRY LENS

Ask:

> **Am I losing the rules by which this reality may change?**

When useful:

```text
DEPENDS_ON
What keeps this judgement, state, or structure sensible?

REOPEN_IF
What changed condition should cause reconsideration?

PROTECTS_AGAINST
What expensive failure helps explain why this exists?

REINTRODUCES
What known failure returns if this changes or disappears?
```

Preserve the connection when losing the connection changes the meaning.

Do not manufacture geometry unsupported by project reality.

### QUESTION LENS

Ask only where useful:

```text
What was expensive to learn?

What changes if this changes?

What should reopen judgement?

What would otherwise need to be:
- rediscovered?
- reverified?
- decided again?
```

Questions activate reasoning. They are not cargo.

### LENS DISCIPLINE

Do not apply every lens to every problem.

Do not classify for completeness.

Do not let labels replace rationale.

Do not let the Lens Workshop determine the shape of the continuity artifact.

If removing a lens does not materially worsen continuity, do not carry it.

The artifact should remain shaped by the work.

---

## MOTTO

**Trust intelligence.**

**Preserve reality.**

**Preserve expensive understanding.**

**Challenge stale authority.**

**Protect recoverability.**

**Adapt to capability.**

**Preserve successor freedom.**

**Continue the work.**
